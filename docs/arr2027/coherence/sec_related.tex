\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
%% ============================================================================

\paragraph{Factuality and consistency.} Claim-level metrics aggregate independent per-claim
verdicts \citep{factscore,safe,veriscore}, over decompositions whose choice matters
\citep{wanner2024}, and FactReasoner \citep{factreasoner} replaces counting with inference over
claims and evidence; \citet{lorefact} show that such pipelines miss errors in the logic that
connects the claims. Factual-consistency metrics check a text against a source
\citep{factcc,summac,alignscore,minicheck}; other detectors flag inconsistencies across samples
\citep{selfcheckgpt}, within one generation \citep{mundler2024} or a dialogue
\citep{welleck2019,decode}, and across a model's answers or beliefs
\citep{ribeiro2019,li2019logic,elazar2021,becel}. Reasoning-chain metrics and verifiers score
explicit derivations step by step \citep{roscoe,receval,entailmentbank,lightman2024}. None of them says
how supports and conflicts should combine into one score, and none fixes a committed world under
which refuting a falsehood counts as support.

\paragraph{Discourse coherence.} It is explained by cohesive ties \citep{hallidayhasan1976}, entity
continuity \citep{grosz1995} or inferred relations \citep{hobbs1979,mann,pdtb}, and scored by entity
grids \citep{barzilay}, neural models \citep{li}, DiscoScore \citep{discoscore}, entity--discourse
graphs \citep{liustrube} and LLM judges \citep{geval,zheng2023judge}, and rated on natural text
\citep{gcdc}, none with a guarantee on how the score responds to the truth constraints between
claims.

\paragraph{Argumentation and logical constraints.} Argument mining recovers support and attack
relations \citep{stab2017}, argument quality and strength are rated for cogency and persuasiveness
\citep{wachsmuth2017,habernal2016,persing2015}, and entailment graphs link propositions
\citep{berant2011}. Gradual semantics for bipolar argumentation grade arguments under axioms such
as neutrality and monotony \citep{cayrol2005,amgoud2016,amgoud2018,baroni2019}, the closest
precedent to our axioms, but they grade acceptability from attacks and supports, not accordance
with a committed truth assignment. Probabilistic argumentation and explanatory coherence constrain
beliefs by attacks or by positive and negative constraints
\citep{thimm2012,hunter2013,thagard1989,thagard1998}. BeliefBank \citep{beliefbank}, ConCoRD \citep{concord}
and Maieutic prompting \citep{maieutic} use weighted MAX-SAT or factor graphs over NLI constraints
to make beliefs consistent, as in Markov logic \citep{mln} and PSL \citep{psl}. We use such a model
to \emph{measure} a response rather than to repair it (App.~\ref{app:why}).

\paragraph{Probabilistic coherence and confirmation.} Bayesian epistemology measures the coherence of
a set of propositions under a joint distribution
\citep{shogenji1999,olsson2002,fitelson2003,douven2007}, with impossibility results on
truth-conduciveness \citep{bovens2003}. Confirmation theory measures how evidence moves belief in a
hypothesis, by the log-ratio \citep{milne1996} or the weight of evidence \citep{good1950}, which are
not ordinally equivalent in general \citep{fitelson1999,crupi2007}. $\LCS$ is the logistic of the
weight of evidence that the relations lend the committed world. Unlike coherence measures, which
ignore the propositions' truth values, it holds the propositions fixed and grades the relations
against a committed world (App.~\ref{app:verify}).
