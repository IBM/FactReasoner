Logical coherence is how well the logical relations a response draws between its claims (inferences, explanations, refutations, contradictions) agree with the claims' truth values. Supporting or deriving a true claim from true claims, and refuting a false claim, are coherent. Setting two true claims against each other, or deriving a false claim from a true one, is incoherent. Do not judge whether the claims are true, and do not judge style or organization: judge only whether the relations the response draws fit the truth values.
