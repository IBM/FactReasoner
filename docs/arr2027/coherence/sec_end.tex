\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:discussion}
%% ============================================================================

We reduced logical coherence to one requirement, monotonicity in the coherence order over a fixed
claim set, and derived the score that meets it: the weight of evidence that the coherence MRF lends
to the committed world, with sign-definite potentials and that world forced. On LoCoBench it
holds exactly on gold graphs and every misranking on mined graphs traces to extraction.
On natural text the score separates logical from discourse coherence, falls under
self-contradiction and tracks essay quality beyond length. What limits it is relation extraction: recall, above all for contradictions
within a sentence, and calibrated weights. Logical coherence is meant to be reported beside
factuality and discourse coherence, not instead of them.

\section*{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}

\textbf{Data.} LoCoBench's responses were written by a single generator (Claude Opus~5) and admitted
by a single committee, so results may reflect one model's writing style. Gold relations are what
the committee read. The ladders are diagnostic, not a sample of how real responses argue: rungs
differ in one or two spliced sentences with explicit markers, and claims are given rather than
extracted. Mined-graph results on LoCoBench are therefore upper bounds on extraction from natural
text. The external evaluation uses natural text with human labels (rated coherence, contradiction
labels, discourse and argument relations, essay scores), but none of these labels rates the
logical coherence of a response, so agreement with human judgements of logical coherence remains
to be established. The essays come from one prompt, were mined with one model, use truth values
from a model committee, and were not pre-registered.
\textbf{Selection.} The committee and the miners share vendors, and explicit fillers favour any
extractor that reads connectives, so mined results on LoCoBench are optimistic.
\textbf{Tolerance.} The tolerant counts depend on $\tau$, fixed at $\sThreeTol$ on development
families; the exact counts do not.
\textbf{Scope of the guarantee.} Monotonicity holds with respect to the relation graph, for a fixed
committed world. When an extractor changes relations in both directions, the score nets them and no
ordering is guaranteed. The score does not compare responses with different claim sets, where
$\LCS$ is a reading on a common scale rather than a guaranteed order. The necessity results fix the
tables and the readout world but not the functional form of the score. Relations on one pair that
contradict each other cancel, so they score as uninformative rather than as incoherent.
\textbf{Polarity.} Verdict polarity inherits the factuality pipeline's errors: a wrong verdict can
flip the sign of the relations at that claim. Verdicts are binary, so a claim the evidence neither
supports nor refutes is treated as false. On LoCoBench the verdicts are the generator's factuality
labels, which model an error-free pipeline, so the effect of verdict errors is not measured.
\textbf{Modelling.} No relation weight is calibrated, and the pairwise miner's weights saturate the
score. Only \sense{Refutation} compiles to \con{}, so an incompatibility conveyed without being
asserted produces no factor. Couplings are material constraints, so relevance of premise to
conclusion is not modelled. Saturation bounds redundant support but cannot stop a miner from
hallucinating new support, so the score inherits the extractor's precision.

\section*{Ethics Statement}

The score is intended for evaluating and diagnosing generated text. A high coherence score is not
evidence of truth, and it should not be used on its own to certify content. LoCoBench is synthetic
and contains no personal data. By design it contains plausible false claims written as expert prose,
and every item carries per-claim factuality labels, which released copies should keep attached.
This paper collects no new human data. The external evaluation uses public datasets under their
licences (App.~\ref{app:external}). The raw files of datasets whose licences restrict or do not
state redistribution are not redistributed. The ASAP~2.0 essays were written by school students and
are released under CC BY 4.0. We use their text and scores only, no demographic metadata, and we do
not redistribute the copyrighted source article. LLMs were used to generate the benchmark
responses, to validate them, to extract claims and relations and as judges, as described. They
were also used for editing assistance in preparing this paper.
