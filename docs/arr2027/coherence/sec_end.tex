\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:discussion}
%% ============================================================================

We asked what a score of logical coherence must satisfy, and answered with one requirement: for a
fixed set of claims and their verdicts, it must rise under every coherence-improving edit of the
argument's relations. $\LCS$, the weight of evidence that the coherence MRF lends to the committed
world, meets it for any priors and any relation graph (Theorem~\ref{thm:mono}), and the requirement
pins down the class of relation tables and the world at which the model is read
(App.~\ref{app:why}). On LoCoBench, $\LCS$ satisfies every declared order on gold graphs, and on
mined graphs it falls only on pairs whose edit the miner reversed or left incomparable; LLM judges,
by contrast, are near chance when the only change is an added valid inference. On natural text,
where no human ratings of logical coherence exist, the score is not a proxy for rated discourse
coherence, falls when a detected contradiction is added, moves no more under paragraph permutation
than under re-mining, and tracks essay quality beyond length. What limits $\LCS$ is relation extraction: recall,
above all for contradictions within a sentence, and calibrated weights. Logical coherence is meant
to be reported beside factuality and discourse coherence, not instead of them.

\section*{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Data.} LoCoBench's responses were written by a single generator (Claude Opus~5) and
admitted by a single committee, so results may reflect one model's writing style. Its gold relations
are the committee's reading of each response, not human annotations. The ladders are diagnostic, not
a sample of how real responses argue: rungs differ in one or two spliced sentences with explicit
markers, and claims are given rather than extracted. The committee and the miners share vendors, and
explicit markers favour any extractor that reads connectives, so mined-graph results on LoCoBench
are optimistic, an upper bound on extraction from natural text.

\paragraph{External evaluation.} The natural-text datasets carry human labels (rated coherence,
contradictions, discourse and argument relations, essay scores), but none rates the logical
coherence of a response, so agreement with human judgements of logical coherence remains to be
established. The order check depends on the tolerance $\tau=\sThreeTol$ of
\S\ref{sec:experiments}, and order invariance of the mined score is shown for GPT-6 only. The essays
come from one prompt and were mined with one model.

\paragraph{Scope of the guarantee.} Monotonicity holds with respect to the relation graph, for a
fixed committed world. When an extractor changes relations in both directions, the score nets them
and no ordering is guaranteed. The score does not compare responses with different claim sets,
where $\LCS$ is a reading on a common scale rather than a guaranteed order. The necessity results
(App.~\ref{app:why}) fix the relation tables and the world at which the model is read, but not the
functional form of the score. Relations on one pair that contradict each other cancel, so they
score as uninformative rather than as incoherent.

\paragraph{Verdicts.} At the verdict world the score inherits the factuality pipeline's errors: a
wrong verdict can flip the sign of the relations at that claim. Verdicts are binary, so a claim the
evidence neither supports nor refutes is treated as false. On LoCoBench the verdicts are the
generator's factuality labels, which model an error-free pipeline, so the effect of verdict errors
is not measured.

\paragraph{Modelling.} Relations join two claims, so a contradiction within a single claim cannot
be represented; on ContraDoc this is where recall is lowest. No relation weight is calibrated, and
the pairwise miner's weights saturate the score. Only \sense{Refutation} compiles to \con{}, so an
incompatibility conveyed without being asserted produces no factor. Couplings are material
constraints, so relevance of premise to conclusion is not modelled. Saturation bounds redundant
support but cannot stop a miner from hallucinating new support, so the score inherits the
extractor's precision.

\paragraph{Computation and reproducibility.} The score needs the partition function $Z_\R$, which
is \#P-hard in general; it is exact here because the graphs are small, and approximate inference
preserves the guarantees only up to the approximation (App.~\ref{app:further}). The strongest miner
and judge, GPT-6, are proprietary models served through an API, so their outputs may not be
reproducible over time.

\section*{Ethics Statement}

The score is intended for evaluating and diagnosing generated text. A high coherence score is not
evidence of truth, and it should not be used on its own to certify content. LoCoBench is synthetic
and contains no personal data. By design it contains plausible false claims written as expert prose,
and every item carries per-claim factuality labels, which released copies should keep attached.
This paper collects no new human data. The external evaluation uses public datasets under their
licences (App.~\ref{app:external}). The raw files of datasets whose licences restrict or do not
state redistribution are not redistributed. The ASAP~2.0 essays were written by school students and
are released under CC BY 4.0. We use their text and scores only, no demographic metadata, and we do
not redistribute the copyrighted source article. LLMs were used to generate the benchmark
responses, to validate them, to extract claims and relations and as judges, as described. They
were also used for editing assistance in preparing this paper.
