\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:discussion}
%% ============================================================================

We required a score of logical coherence to rise under every coherence-improving edit of an
argument's relations, for fixed claims and verdicts. $\LCS$, the weight of evidence that the coherence
MRF lends to the committed world, meets this for any priors and relation graph (Theorem~\ref{thm:mono}),
and within product MRFs read at one world it forces sign-definite tables and the committed world as
the readout (App.~\ref{app:why}).

$\LCS$ splits into per-relation evidence and an interaction term (Prop.~\ref{prop:decomp}), and its
first-order expansion, the linear evidence, is the score we recommend, on theoretical grounds: on our
data, once ties count as half, it is not separable from $\LCS$ or from a plain signed sum. The full model treats repeated and contested evidence exactly, but its interaction ignores the
verdicts and needs human judgments to justify it.
A judge comparing two responses with their verdicts trails $\LCS$ slightly on increases and is more stable
on invariances. On natural text, read with every claim asserted, scores of mined relations fall under detected
contradictions, track essay quality beyond length about as well as a relation count, and relate to
rated discourse coherence only through which relations are mined. What limits the score is relation extraction, the verdicts it is read against, and two design
choices: restating a claim earns credit, and relevance is not modeled. Logical coherence is meant
to be reported beside factuality and discourse coherence, not instead of them.

\section*{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Data.} LoCoBench's responses were written by a single generator (Claude Opus~5) and
admitted by a single committee, so results may reflect one model's writing style. Its gold relations
are the committee's reading of each response, not human annotations. The ladders are diagnostic, not
a sample of how real responses argue: rungs differ in one or two spliced sentences with explicit
markers, and claims are given rather than extracted. The admission gate is defined in $\Lambda$, so gold-graph results hold by construction and favor any
score monotone in the coherence order. The committee and the miners share vendors (GPT-6.1 sits on the
committee and GPT-6 mines), and
explicit markers favor any extractor that reads connectives, so mined-graph results on LoCoBench
are optimistic, an upper bound on extraction from natural text. A graph-level miner from outside
those vendors (Llama-3.3-70B, on \subLadders{} ladders) reaches a pair-level F1 of \subLlamaPairF{}
against \subGptPairF{} for GPT-6, and $\LCS$ on its graphs falls on \subLlamaLcsFalls\% of the
increases.

\paragraph{External evaluation.} The natural-text datasets carry human labels (rated coherence,
contradictions, discourse and argument relations, essay scores), but none rates the logical
coherence of a response, so agreement with human judgments of logical coherence remains to be
established, and no human study checks that people rank LoCoBench's rungs as declared. Most
natural-text tests read the asserted world. Where the verdict world was tested on natural text, it did not help: ContraDoc's preregistered
verdict-world test failed (App.~\ref{app:external}); on ChatProtect the score rises on all
\extCpVwFoundNGpt{} found contradictions, close to true by Lemma~\ref{lem:edit}, but on \extCpVwAllIncGpt{} against \extCpVwAllDecGpt{} of all
\extCpVwAllNGpt{} pairs ($p=\extCpVwAllPGpt$); and on essays the strict world lowers the partial
correlation (App.~\ref{app:asap}). With the gpt-oss-120b miner the ChatProtect test is null
(\extCpAssertDecOss{} falls, \extCpAssertIncOss{} rises) and ContraDoc recall is \extCdRecallInsertOss\%. The ExpertQA analysis uses one expert's labels per answer, counts unsure and unlabelled claims as
false, and uses answers selected to contain an incorrect claim, which all raise the number of
violations.
At first order a restatement, an equivalence, earns twice the credit of an inference, so the score
rewards restating a claim, which raters penalize. The score is
order-free, but its extraction is only nearly so: on LoCoBench, permuting paragraphs moves the mined
score slightly beyond the miner's re-mining noise. The essays come from one prompt, sampled uniformly over score
levels, and were mined with one model.

\paragraph{Scope of the guarantee.} Monotonicity holds with respect to the relation graph, for a
fixed committed world. When an extractor changes relations in both directions, the score nets them
and no ordering is guaranteed. The guarantee does not cover responses with different claim sets: there, as in \S\ref{sec:exp-external},
$\LCS$ is a reading on a common scale rather than a guaranteed order. The necessity results
(App.~\ref{app:why}) fix the relation tables and the world at which the model is read, but not the
functional form of the score. Relations on one pair that contradict each other cancel, so they
score as uninformative rather than as incoherent.

\paragraph{Verdicts.} At the verdict world the score inherits the factuality pipeline's errors: a
wrong verdict can flip the sign of the relations at that claim. On LoCoBench's gold graphs, flipping
one verdict of a claim with a relation moves $\LCS$ by a median of \flipMedian{} (more than $0.1$
for \flipOverTenth\% of the \flipTouched{} such flips), and the change grows with the claim's
degree. Flipping each verdict with probability $0.2$, $\LCS$ still satisfies \vnLcsIncTwenty\% of
LoCoBench's increases on gold graphs, against \vnWsumIncTwenty\% for the signed sum, and with a
closed-book GPT-6 verifier (\vfGptAcc\% accurate) it satisfies \vfGptGoldLcsInc\% against
\vfGptGoldWsumInc\%; on mined graphs the difference is \asapCI{\vfGptMinedDiffloInc}{\vfGptMinedDiffhiInc} points, and $\LCS$
falls more often (\vfGptMinedLcsfallInc\% against \vfGptMinedWsumfallInc\%). Verdicts are binary, so a claim the
evidence neither supports nor refutes is treated as false. On LoCoBench the verdicts are the
generator's factuality labels, an error-free pipeline; App.~\ref{app:results} measures injected and
verifier errors.

\paragraph{Modeling.} Relations join two claims, so a contradiction within a single claim cannot
be represented; on ContraDoc this is where recall is lowest. No relation weight is calibrated, and
the pairwise miner's weights saturate the score. Only \sense{Refutation} compiles to \con{}, so an
incompatibility conveyed without being asserted produces no factor. Couplings are material
constraints, so relevance of premise to conclusion is not modeled: a non-sequitur between two true
claims, stated explicitly, is extracted in \nsExtracted\% of LoCoBench responses and raises $\LCS$ in
\nsLcsRise\%, about as often as a valid inference does (\nvLcsRise\%). The interaction term ignores the
verdicts, so a relation that refutes a falsehood by citing a claim discounts the other inferences
drawn from that claim, as one that contests the claim would (\S\ref{sec:decomp}); this happens on
LoCoBench (App.~\ref{app:decomp}), and we have no argument that it is desirable. $\LCS$ saturates redundant support but the recommended $\Llin$ does not: it counts a repeated
support in full, so a response padded with restated supports scores higher; neither can stop a miner
from hallucinating new support, so the score inherits the extractor's precision.

\paragraph{Computation and reproducibility.} The score needs the partition function $Z_\R$, which
is \#P-hard in general; it is exact here because the graphs are small, and approximate inference
preserves the guarantees only up to the approximation (App.~\ref{app:further}). The recommended linear evidence $\Llin$ needs no $Z_\R$ and costs $O(|\R|)$; it keeps the asymmetry,
whose measured benefit on our data is mostly tie-breaking, and loses the interaction and with it part
of A2
(\S\ref{sec:decomp}). The strongest miner
and judge, GPT-6, are proprietary models served through an API, so their outputs may not be
reproducible over time.

\section*{Ethics Statement}

The score is intended for evaluating and diagnosing generated text. A high coherence score is not
evidence of truth, and it should not be used on its own to certify content. LoCoBench is synthetic
and contains no personal data. By design it contains plausible false claims written as expert prose,
and every item carries per-claim factuality labels, which released copies should keep attached.
This paper collects no new human data. The external evaluation uses public datasets under their licenses (App.~\ref{app:external}); the
licenses cover the datasets as released, and the underlying texts (news, Wikipedia, model outputs)
keep their own terms. The raw files of datasets whose licenses restrict or do not
state redistribution are not redistributed. GDTB is released under CC BY-NC-SA and is used for
research only; QT30 states no license, so we use it for research only and redistribute nothing from
it. The ASAP~2.0 essays were written by school students in grades 6--10 and are released under CC BY
4.0, which permits this use; we use their text and scores only, no demographic metadata. These essays, written by minors, were
sent to commercial model APIs under the providers' standard API terms for claim extraction, relation
mining and source checking. We do not redistribute the
copyrighted source article, which was transcribed only for the source check and, like the texts of
every external dataset, sent to the same model APIs. LLMs were used to generate the benchmark
responses, to validate them, to extract claims and relations and as judges, as described. They
were also used for editing assistance in preparing this paper.
