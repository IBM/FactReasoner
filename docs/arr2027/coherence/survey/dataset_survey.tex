\title{External Datasets for Evaluating the Logical Coherence Score\\
\large A survey and evaluation plan, companion to the coherence paper}
\author{Internal report}
\date{23 November 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
\noindent We surveyed about seventy datasets in four areas: argumentation, reasoning chains,
contradiction and long-form factuality, and discourse coherence. The question was whether any of
them could evaluate the logical coherence score (LCS) and its pipeline, as a complement to the
synthetic LoCoBench ladders. None can replace LoCoBench. No released dataset combines multi-claim
text, gold relations between claims that include \emph{both} supports and conflicts, and a truth
label for each claim. Each candidate has at most two of the three. The closest prior work, LoReFact
\citep{lorefact}, labels relations only within single sentences and has not released its data. Each
part of the pipeline can still be tested on existing data: correlation with human judgements,
relation mining, pairs whose expected effect on the score is known, and the dependence of a
relation's sign on truth. Discourse-coherence data serve as negative controls. We recommend running
these checks on released data first, and then annotating relations on a small set of natural LLM
responses that already carry human truth labels for each claim, which would fill the gap the survey
found.
\end{abstract}

%% ============================================================================
\section{What a Dataset Would Need}
%% ============================================================================

The score is computed in four steps:
\begin{enumerate}
\item A response is decomposed into atomic claims.
\item A miner extracts the relations between claims, each with a discourse sense.
\item Each sense is compiled to a truth-functional coupling: entailment, contradiction,
  equivalence, exclusion, co-necessity, or none. \sense{Contrast}, \sense{Concession} and the
  temporal senses compile to none.
\item $\LCS$ reads a Markov random field over the claims' truth values at the committed world fixed
  by a factuality pipeline.
\end{enumerate}
Several properties follow from this construction. $\LCS$ rises when a support is added or a
conflict removed. A relation's sign depends on the truth values. The score does not change when the
text is reordered or reworded.

Every candidate was scored on six requirements:
\begin{description}[font=\normalfont\bfseries,leftmargin=2.2em,style=sameline]
\item[R1] multi-claim text, LLM-generated if possible;
\item[R2] claims, propositions or steps already segmented;
\item[R3] gold relations between units whose types map to couplings;
\item[R4] a truth or factuality label for each unit;
\item[R5] controlled pairs whose effect is declared, or human judgements of logical quality at the
  level of the whole text;
\item[R6] public availability, with licence, size and language.
\end{description}
Candidates were also classified by the uses they support:
\begin{description}[font=\normalfont\bfseries,leftmargin=2.2em,style=sameline]
\item[U1] correlating the score with human judgements;
\item[U2] evaluating the relation miner against gold relations;
\item[U3] tests on pairs with a declared effect;
\item[U4] tests of the sign of a relation, which depends on truth;
\item[NC] negative controls, contrasts the score should \emph{not} respond to.
\end{description}
LoCoBench meets R1--R5 by construction but is synthetic. An external dataset is useful if it meets
R3 or R4 together with R5 on natural text.

\section{Closest Prior Work: LoReFact}
%% ============================================================================

LoReFact \citep{lorefact} has 1{,}080 GPT-4o responses of about 819 words each, with 5{,}706
``logical statements''. Each statement carries three labels:
\begin{itemize}
\item a relation type: causal 3{,}358, conditional 1{,}551, temporal 473 or concessive 324;
\item a binary label for whether the dependency holds;
\item a truth label for each claim it contains.
\end{itemize}
It is the only prior work that judges logic and factuality together. It differs from ours in four
ways:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Its relations stay within a single sentence, and it builds no graph; the authors note that it
  does not guarantee global consistency across multi-step chains.
\item It has no contradiction, equivalence or exclusion.
\item 78\% of its statements are deliberately flawed. The generation also often produces cases where
  $A$ and $B$ are both true but ``because'' is false. That is a question of whether the relation is
  valid, not a constraint on truth values.
\item It labels concessive statements, which our compile map sends to \emph{none}.
\end{enumerate}
Its GitHub repository is empty\ok{} (size 0, last push 1 October 2025), so we would have to obtain
the data from the authors. It is the work to contrast with in both papers.

\section{Candidates by Use}
%% ============================================================================

\paragraph{U1: human judgements.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Xu et al.} \citep{xu2023lfqa}: 854 pairwise coherence preferences, with rationales, on
  long-form answers. This is the best target on long-form text. Their notion of coherence includes
  organization, so expect a modest correlation.
\item \textbf{Dagstuhl-15512} \citep{wachsmuth2017}: expert ratings of cogency and local
  sufficiency, the closest human dimensions to what $\LCS$ measures. Agreement is low.
\item \textbf{REVEAL} \citep{reveal}: logic labels for each step and for the whole chain, on LLM
  chains of thought.
\item \textbf{CoheSentia} \citep{cohesentia}: per-sentence ``Consistency'' reasons. Read it at the
  asserted world, since fiction has no external facts. Its Cohesion and Relevance reasons give a
  discriminant check.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{U2: relation miner.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{QT30 / IAT} \citep{qt30,us2016}: Inference, Rephrase and Conflict map onto entailment,
  equivalence and contradiction. This is the cleanest external gold data for all three main
  couplings. Caveats: it is dialogue, many Rephrase relations link a question to its answer, and it
  has no truth labels.
\item \textbf{Arg-Microtexts, multilayer version} \citep{microtexts,microtexts2}: RST and SDRT senses
  (including \sense{Concession} and \sense{Contrast}) sit on the same segments as support and attack
  relations. This tests the compile map directly. Undercut attacks an inference rather than a claim,
  so it should receive no claim-level coupling.
\item \textbf{Discourse treebanks} \citep{pdtb,rstdt,gum}: score the miner at the coupling level
  after mapping senses to couplings. GDTB is the open option; from DISRPT 2025, keep the original
  labels. These texts hardly contain any contradiction.
\item \textbf{Contradiction only:} Mündler et al.\ \citep{mundler2024} and ContraDoc
  \citep{contradoc}.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{U3: pairs with a declared effect.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{ContraDoc}: its clean and contradicted documents are not paired, but each of the
  178 Insert cases gives a pair once the inserted sentence, which appears verbatim, is deleted.
  At the asserted world, the contradiction should lower $\LCS$. At the verdict world, a true
  sentence set against a false inserted one is a support, so $\LCS$ may rise. The effect can be
  stratified by scope (global, local, within one sentence).
\item \textbf{Mündler et al.}: splicing the resampled alternative sentence into the response gives a
  natural minimal pair.
\item \textbf{ROSCOE and ReCEval perturbations of EntailmentBank} \citep{roscoe,receval}:
  \begin{itemize}
  \item negating a step should lower $\LCS$;
  \item an unrelated hallucinated step should not move it;
  \item shuffle and swap should leave it \emph{unchanged}.
  \end{itemize}
  ROSCOE counts shuffle and swap as coherence errors. That disagreement is worth reporting.
\item \textbf{RobustLR} \citep{robustlr}: its contrast sets flip a label and its equivalence sets
  preserve one.
\item \textbf{ARCT} \citep{arct}: pairs of opposite warrants.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{U4: the sign of a relation follows the truth values.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{BeliefBank} \citep{beliefbank,concord} is the cleanest test.
  \begin{enumerate}
  \item Verbalize about ten facts per entity as a response.
  \item Flip one fact's truth value.
  \item Read off from the gold implication and exclusion constraints which relations change sign.
  \end{enumerate}
  The expected sign of the change in $\LCS$ is then known in advance.
\item \textbf{Mündler et al.:} in 63\% of ChatGPT's self-contradictions both sentences are false.
  There, $\LCS$ should differ from simply counting contradictions.
\item \textbf{LIARArg:} support and attack relations around a claim whose veracity is known. Its
  release is unverified.
\item \textbf{REVEAL}'s contradictory steps and \textbf{PARC}'s accumulation errors \citep{parc}
  are analogues of deriving a falsehood.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Negative controls.} Under sentence and paragraph shuffles \citep{barzilay}, $\LCS$ should
not move. It should also be close to uncorrelated with the coherence ratings of GCDC \citep{gcdc},
SummEval \citep{summeval} and HANNA \citep{hanna}, which rate organization and readability.
SummEval's \emph{Consistency} dimension is faithfulness to the source document. $\LCS$ deliberately
does not measure that, so it is not a positive target either.

\section{Recommendations}
%% ============================================================================

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Cheap checks on released data.} Each check tests one property:
  \begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Negative controls:} shuffled LoCoBench and ContraDoc documents, plus the
    discourse-coherence ratings above.
  \item \emph{ContraDoc pairs:} U3, and contradiction recall of the miner for U2.
  \item \emph{Mündler et al.\ pairs:} U4.
  \item \emph{Relation-miner evaluation on QT30:} U2, with GDTB for the senses that compile to
    \emph{none}.
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{Fill the gap.} Annotate claim-to-claim relations on 40--60 natural LLM answers
  from ExpertQA \citep{expertqa}, which already carry expert truth labels for each claim. The pool
  is 165 answers of at least 150 words and 6 claims with at least one incorrect claim, across 25
  fields. Factcheck-Bench \citep{factcheckbench} and FELM \citep{felm} were considered and set
  aside: their responses are short, and FELM labels segments rather than claims. This would be the
  first natural dataset with gold relation graphs and gold truth values. It would also show how
  often these relations occur in real output, which LoCoBench cannot.
\item \textbf{Moderate effort, later.} Correlate with the preferences of Xu et al.\ and with
  REVEAL's labels. Run sign-flip tests on verbalized BeliefBank. Run the EntailmentBank
  perturbations.
\item \textbf{Ask the LoReFact authors for their data.}
\end{enumerate}

\section{Implications for the Two Papers}
%% ============================================================================

The survey supports the claim in the LoCoBench paper that natural corpora provide neither minimal
pairs nor gold graphs: no released corpus has gold relation graphs that include conflicts together
with truth labels for each claim. Related Work in both papers should add three things:
\begin{itemize}
\item LoReFact, the closest framing of the task;
\item QT30 and its argumentation scheme, the closest to our couplings;
\item ROSCOE's convention that shuffling is an error, as a point of explicit disagreement.
\end{itemize}
