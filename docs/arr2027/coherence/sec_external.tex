\section{External Evaluation}
\label{sec:exp-external}
%% ============================================================================

LoCoBench is synthetic, so we also tested the score on natural text that others built and labelled:
ContraDoc \citep{contradoc} (documents with an inserted self-contradiction), SummEval
\citep{summeval} (summaries with expert coherence ratings), ChatProtect \citep{mundler2024} (LLM
self-contradictions), QT30 \citep{qt30} (debates annotated with inference and conflict) and GDTB
\citep{gdtb} (PDTB-style discourse relations), with paragraph-shuffled LoCoBench responses for the
order check. Claims are sentences (QT30: propositions; GDTB: relation arguments). Relations come
from the graph-level miner with GPT-6 and with gpt-oss-120b, and $\LCS$ uses flat priors and,
unless stated, the asserted world. We test seven \emph{advance predictions} about the score, each
written down with its test and threshold before any graph of these checks was mined
(App.~\ref{app:external}). Table~\ref{tab:external} reports all seven, held or not. Other numbers
in this section are diagnostics computed afterwards to explain the outcomes, and the essay study at
the end goes beyond the advance predictions.

\paragraph{What held.} Five of the seven predictions hold with GPT-6 and four with gpt-oss-120b.
$\LCS$ is uncorrelated with SummEval's expert coherence ratings, which G-Eval tracks (E1). An
inserted or self-contradicting sentence lowers it (E3, E4), and where the miner finds the
contradiction it falls on \extCdAssertEdgeDecGpt{} of \extCdAssertEdgeNGpt{} ContraDoc pairs and on
all \extCpFoundFallGpt{} ChatProtect pairs, so almost every rise comes from a missed contradiction. On
ContraDoc's clean documents, \extCdFpGpt\% of graphs contain a contradiction. Every relation's
contribution takes the sign the theory predicts (E5). At ChatProtect's real verdicts, the
contradictions found are \extCpRealSupportGpt{} supports (a sentence of the pair is false) and
\extCpRealConflictGpt{} conflicts, all of which a contradiction count would charge. On GDTB,
contrast, concession and temporal relations rarely receive a coupling, as the compile map requires
(E7).

\paragraph{What failed, and why.} E2 failed with both arms, because the baselines that win are
deterministic functions of the text, while the miner samples. Gold-graph $\LCS$ is identical under
every permutation, and the GPT-6 score moves no more under permutation than when the unchanged text
is mined again (\extLocoPermInvGpt\% against \extLocoNoiseInvGpt\% within $\tau$ on LoCoBench,
\extCdPermInvGpt\% against \extCdNoiseInvGpt\% on ContraDoc; Figure~\ref{fig:external}a). The judge,
by contrast, falls on \extLocoPermDecJudge\% and \extCdPermDecJudge\% of the permutations, as if
reordering were incoherence. For gpt-oss-120b, order matters somewhat beyond its noise. E6 failed
with both arms: the score rises more often than it falls, as predicted, but not significantly. The
two versions of a document are mined separately, and none of the \extCdVerdFallsGpt{} falls has a
conflict on the inserted sentence, while all \extCdEvSupGpt{} relations drawn to it are supports.
E4 also fails with gpt-oss-120b, which finds only \extCpConfStrongOss\% of ChatProtect's
contradictions. Of three further predictions about the miner alone (App.~\ref{app:external}), two
held, and the third, that recall falls with the distance between the two sentences, failed: GPT-6
finds \extCdRecallIntraGpt\% of contradictions within one sentence, which no relation between two
claims can represent. Throughout, the binding constraint is relation recall. GPT-6 finds the
contradiction in \extCdRecallGpt\% of ContraDoc's contradicted documents (gpt-oss-120b:
\extCdRecallOss\%), and on QT30 it recovers \extQtRecEntailmentGpt\% of the annotated inferences but
only \extQtRecContradictionGpt\% of the conflicts.

\paragraph{Essay quality.} Beyond the advance predictions, we asked whether $\LCS$ tracks the
quality of natural arguments, on \asapEssays{} persuasive essays from ASAP~2.0
\citep{crossley2025asap} scored 1--6 by trained raters. The full pipeline runs, from atomizing to
mining (GPT-6), outside the guarantee, since every essay has its own claims. $\LCS$ keeps a partial
correlation of $\asapParLcsAsserted$ [\asapParLcsAssertedLo, \asapParLcsAssertedHi] with the score
once length is removed, about as much as G-Eval ($\asapParJudgeGevalGptSixSol$), and
$\asapPwjLcsAsserted$ once G-Eval is removed as well (Figure~\ref{fig:external}c). The signal is the
density of supported reasoning: the relation count carries most of it ($\asapParRelations$ after
length), and the miner finds only \asapConfAsserted{} conflicts in \asapConfEssaysAsserted{} of the
essays. Counting claims absent from the source article as false lowers the partial correlation to
$\asapParLcsStrict$ (App.~\ref{app:asap}).
