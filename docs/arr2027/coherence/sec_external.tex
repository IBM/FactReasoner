LoCoBench is synthetic, and no corpus rates the logical coherence of a response, so we test four
properties the framework implies on natural text that others labelled for a related property
(Table~\ref{tab:external}). Claims are sentences (atomized claims for the essays), relations come
from the GPT-6 graph-level miner, and $\LCS$ is read at the asserted world, where every claim is
true: these datasets give no per-claim verdicts, and at that world every detected contradiction is
a conflict. Brackets give 95\% bootstrap intervals. App.~\ref{app:external}
has the data, a figure, the gpt-oss-120b arm and further analyses.

\paragraph{Not a proxy for discourse coherence.} $\LCS$ reads only the relations that constrain
the claims' truth values (App.~\ref{sec:notions}), not their order or fluency. On SummEval
\citep{summeval} its correlation with the experts' coherence ratings is $\extSeRhoCohGpt$
\asapCI{\extSeRhoCohGptLo}{\extSeRhoCohGptHi}, against $\extSeJudgeRhoCoh$ for G-Eval, but
\extSeEmptyGpt\% of the summaries have no relation and score exactly $\frac12$. On the
\extSeNonEmptyNGpt{} that have one, it is $\extSeNonEmptyRhoCohGpt$
\asapCI{\extSeNonEmptyRhoCohGptLo}{\extSeNonEmptyRhoCohGptHi}, and the two notions part for a
principled reason: a restatement of an asserted claim is consistent and counts as a support, but
raters penalize the redundancy it signals (the number of restatements correlates with rated
coherence at $\extSeCountRestateRhoCohGpt$). On graphs with inferences only, $\LCS$ correlates
weakly and positively with rated coherence ($\extSeInfOnlyRhoCohGpt$
\asapCI{\extSeInfOnlyRhoCohGptLo}{\extSeInfOnlyRhoCohGptHi}), as two related notions should.

\paragraph{Detected conflicts lower the score.} With every claim true, adding a contradiction
lowers $\Lambda$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:edit}); the added sentence is also a new claim, so this is a
prediction rather than the theorem (App.~\ref{app:ext-contra}). ContraDoc \citep{contradoc} inserts
a contradicting sentence into a document, and ChatProtect \citep{mundler2024} extends an LLM's
sentence with a self-contradicting one. Where the miner finds the contradiction, $\LCS$ falls on
\extCdAssertEdgeDecGpt{} of \extCdAssertEdgeNGpt{} ContraDoc pairs and on all \extCpFoundFallGpt{}
ChatProtect pairs. Over all pairs it falls far more often than it rises (sign tests,
$p<\extCdAssertPGpt$ and $p<\extCpAssertPGpt$), and almost every rise is a contradiction the miner
missed: detection, not scoring, is the bottleneck. The miner finds \extCdRecallInsertGpt\% of ContraDoc's
inserted contradictions and \extCpConfStrongGpt\% of ChatProtect's. The judge falls too, so contradiction detection
alone does not separate $\LCS$ from a judge; order and LoCoBench do.

\paragraph{Order leaves the score unchanged.} Permuting self-contained paragraphs changes neither
the claims, nor their relations, nor the committed world, so gold-graph $\LCS$ is identical under
every permutation of LoCoBench responses. A mined score also carries the miner's sampling noise,
and permuting moves it no more than mining the unchanged text again: it stays within
$\tau=\sThreeTol$ on \extLocoPermInvGpt\% of LoCoBench permutations against \extLocoNoiseInvGpt\%
for re-mining, and on \extCdPermInvGpt\% of ContraDoc's long documents against \extCdNoiseInvGpt\%.
The judge treats reordering as incoherence, falling on \extLocoPermDecJudge\% and
\extCdPermDecJudge\% of the permutations. With gpt-oss-120b, permutation moves the score beyond
its noise floor on ContraDoc, so this property is shown for GPT-6 (App.~\ref{app:ext-order}).

\paragraph{Essay quality beyond length.} On \asapEssays{} persuasive essays from ASAP~2.0
\citep{crossley2025asap}, scored 1--6 by trained raters, the full pipeline runs, from claim
extraction to mining, and the guarantee does not apply, since every essay has its own claims.
Length correlates with the score at \asapRhoWords. $\LCS$ keeps a partial correlation of
\asapParLcsAsserted{} \asapCI{\asapParLcsAssertedLo}{\asapParLcsAssertedHi} once length is
removed, against \asapParJudgeGevalGptSixSol{} for G-Eval, and \asapPwjLcsAsserted{} once G-Eval
is removed too. The signal is the density of supported reasoning (the number of relations keeps
\asapParRelations{} after length), not conflicts, which the miner finds in only
\asapConfEssaysAsserted{} essays. The essays answer one prompt and were mined with one model, so
the result is indicative (App.~\ref{app:asap}).
