LoCoBench is synthetic and no corpus rates logical coherence, so we test four properties of $\LCS$
on natural text labeled for related properties (Table~\ref{tab:external}). Claims are sentences
(atomized claims for the essays) and relations come from the GPT-6 graph-level miner. Except where
stated, $\LCS$ is read at the asserted world, where every inference is a support and every
contradiction a conflict, so these tests exercise how the score combines mined relations; brackets
give 95\% bootstrap intervals, and the tests fixed before mining are in Table~\ref{tab:ext-plan}. $\LCS$'s three simplifications behave like it on every row. At the verdict world, ContraDoc's test fixed in advance failed ($p=\extCdVerdEdgePGpt$; App.~\ref{app:ext-further}).

\paragraph{Not a proxy for discourse coherence.} $\LCS$ reads only the relations that constrain
truth values (App.~\ref{sec:notions}). This is a discriminant check, which a constant score would
also pass; it shows only what the score is not. On SummEval \citep{summeval} it correlates with expert
coherence ratings at $\extSeRhoCohGpt$ \asapCI{\extSeRhoCohGptLo}{\extSeRhoCohGptHi}, against
$\extSeJudgeRhoCoh$ for G-Eval; \extSeEmptyGpt\% of these three-sentence summaries have no relation,
and on the rest the correlation is negative, $\extSeNonEmptyRhoCohGpt$
\asapCI{\extSeNonEmptyRhoCohGptLo}{\extSeNonEmptyRhoCohGptHi}. Restatements drive it (exploratory): a
restatement is consistent, so $\LCS$ counts it as support, but raters penalize the redundancy, and
their number correlates with rated coherence at $\extSeCountRestateRhoCohGpt$. Without them $\LCS$
correlates at $\extSeNoRestateNonEmptyRhoCohGpt$, no more than the number of inferences
($\extSeCountInfRhoCohGpt$). $\LCS$ thus rewards repetition.

\paragraph{Detected conflicts lower the score.} With every claim true, adding a contradiction
lowers $\Lambda$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:edit}), so where the miner finds one the fall is guaranteed and
checks the lemma end to end:
$\LCS$ falls on \extCdAssertEdgeDecGpt{} of \extCdAssertEdgeNGpt{} ContraDoc pairs
\citep{contradoc} and on all \extCpFoundFallGpt{} ChatProtect pairs \citep{mundler2024} (median
change $\extCdEffFoundMedianGpt$ and $\extCpEffFoundMedianGpt$). Over all pairs it falls far more often than it rises, also by document and topic (App.~\ref{app:ext-contra}), and a consistent sentence raises it (\extCpCtlIncGpt{} rises, \extCpCtlDecGpt{} falls). Missed
contradictions barely move it (on ContraDoc \extCdAssertNotFoundDecGpt{} falls against
\extCdAssertNotFoundIncGpt{} rises), so recall bounds the result: the miner finds
\extCdRecallInsertGpt\% of ContraDoc's inserted contradictions (\extCdRecallGpt\% over all edits) and
\extCpConfStrongGpt\% of ChatProtect's. The simplifications behave alike (no paired difference with the signed sum survives Holm, an
absence of evidence rather than an equivalence), and the judge falls too.

\paragraph{Order.} Mined twice per text in one run, $\LCS$ moves slightly more under paragraph
permutation than under re-mining (90\% intervals of the difference in mean change: LoCoBench
\extLmEqLoGpt{} to \extLmEqHiGpt, ContraDoc by section \extSecEqLoGpt{} to \extSecEqHiGpt), with
test-retest ICC \extLmIccLcsAllGpt{} and \extSecIccLcsAllGpt{} (judge \extLmIccJudgeAll{} and
\extSecIccJudgeAll; App.~\ref{app:ext-order}). The judge falls on \extLmJudgePermDec\% and
\extSecJudgePermDec\% of permutations, against \extLmJudgeNoiseDec\% and \extSecJudgeNoiseDec\% when
re-judged. Those falls need not be instability: G-Eval rates discourse coherence, which permuting paragraphs is meant to damage. The order tests fixed in advance failed for $\LCS$ too (Table~\ref{tab:ext-plan}; App.~\ref{app:ext-order}).

\paragraph{Essay quality beyond length.} We score \asapEssays{} ASAP~2.0 essays
\citep{crossley2025asap}, rated 1--6 by trained raters, with the full pipeline.
Length correlates with the score at \asapRhoWords. $\LCS$ keeps a partial correlation of
\asapParLcsAsserted{} \asapCI{\asapParLcsAssertedLo}{\asapParLcsAssertedHi} once length is removed
(\asapRobCubic{} to \asapRobBinsSix{} with nonlinear controls), the relation count
\asapParRelations, the signed sum \asapParWsumAsserted{} and G-Eval \asapParJudgeGevalGptSixSol, with no
difference resolved: the essay signal is carried by mined relations in general, not by how
$\LCS$ combines them. A grader prompted with the rubric on a 0--100
scale does better (\asapParJudgeRubricOneZeroZeroGptSixSol), yet $\LCS$ keeps \asapPwrhLcsAsserted{}
\asapCI{\asapPwrhLcsAssertedLo}{\asapPwrhLcsAssertedHi} after length and the grader; it is not
resolvably better than the bare relation count (one prompt, one miner; App.~\ref{app:asap}).
