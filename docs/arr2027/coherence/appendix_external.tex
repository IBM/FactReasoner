\section{External Evaluation in Detail}
\label{app:external}
%% ============================================================================

\paragraph{Protocol.} Table~\ref{tab:ext-data} lists the data. Every prediction, test and threshold was fixed in a pre-registration
committed before any graph of these checks was mined. The settings are:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
\item the graph-level miner with GPT-6 and with gpt-oss-120b, two arms ($k=5$, the taxonomy of Table~\ref{tab:couplings}, the gateway's response
  cache bypassed);
\item $\LCS$ and $\Lambda$ with flat priors, $\Lambda$ exact by variable elimination on each
  connected component;
\item invariance within $\tau=0.01$;
\item two-sided exact sign tests and 95\% bootstrap intervals over the natural group (family,
  source document or nodeset).
\end{itemize}
The baselines are those of \S\ref{sec:exp-baselines} that read a single text: DiscoScore RC and
LC, the entity graph and G-Eval with GPT-6, averaged over three runs (the pre-registered number,
against five in \S\ref{sec:exp-baselines}, for the larger corpora). Two deviations were logged
after the first results were read; both add an analysis and remove none.
\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt,font=\normalfont\itshape]
\item[D1.] DiscoScore RC and LC count noun repetitions regardless of sentence order, so, like the
  length controls the pre-registration excluded, they are order-free by definition. They never
  change under a LoCoBench permutation, and on ContraDoc they stay within $\tau$ on
  \extCdPermInvRc\% and \extCdPermInvLc\% of permutations, presumably because permuting a long
  document changes its sentence segmentation. H1a and H1c fail with them excluded too
  (\extHOneAExclGpt{} and \extHOneCExclGpt{} with GPT-6): the entity graph keeps
  \extLocoPermInvEntity\% and \extCdPermInvEntity\% within $\tau$.
\item[D2.] ContraDoc's clean documents were re-mined with fresh samples, to measure the miner's
  noise floor, as the LoCoBench records already allow.
\end{description}
The analyses marked \emph{exploratory} below were not pre-registered, and neither was the essay
study (App.~\ref{app:asap}). Table~\ref{tab:ext-hyp} lists every
pre-registered hypothesis with its outcome, including those that failed.

\paragraph{Order.} Gold-graph $\LCS$ is identical across every permutation, as it must be: the
claims and relations do not change. With the GPT-6 arm, the mined score moves about as much as
re-mining moves it (Table~\ref{tab:ext-order}):
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
\item on LoCoBench, a mean absolute change of \extLocoPermMeanGpt{} under permutation; beyond
  $\tau$ it falls on \extLocoPermDownTolGpt{} and rises on \extLocoPermUpTolGpt{} of
  \extLocoPermNGpt{} permutations;
\item on ContraDoc, a mean change of \extCdPermMeanGpt{} under permutation against
  \extCdNoiseMeanGpt{} under re-mining.
\end{itemize}
gpt-oss-120b is noisier, and permutation moves its score more than re-mining does: a mean change
of \extCdPermMeanOss{} against \extCdNoiseMeanOss{} on ContraDoc. For that miner, part of the
movement comes from the order in which it reads the text.
The noise grows with document length (median 39 claims on ContraDoc against 14 on LoCoBench),
because five sampled graphs over many claims agree less often. More samples per document would
lower it. Sentence shuffles move the mined score slightly more (\extLocoSentInvGpt\% within
$\tau$), as expected: they break the anaphora the miner reads. The G-Eval judge falls on
\extLocoPermDecJudge\% of LoCoBench permutations, \extLocoSentDecJudge\% of sentence shuffles
and \extCdPermDecJudge\% of ContraDoc permutations.

\paragraph{SummEval.} $\LCS$ with every claim true correlates with the expert ratings as follows:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
\item coherence, $\extSeRhoCohGpt$ {\scriptsize[\extSeRhoCohGptLo,\,\extSeRhoCohGptHi]};
\item consistency, $\extSeRhoConsistencyGpt$;
\item fluency, $\extSeRhoFluencyGpt$;
\item relevance, $\extSeRhoRelevanceGpt$.
\end{itemize}
SummEval's ``consistency'' is faithfulness to the source article, which the score does not
measure. \extSeEmptyGpt\% of the summaries, about three sentences long, have no factor relation
at all, so the check is weak by construction: it shows that the score does not track rated
coherence, not that it tracks anything else. The G-Eval judge, by contrast, correlates with the
same coherence ratings at $\rho=\extSeJudgeRhoCoh$, as a discourse-coherence measure should.

\paragraph{Insertion pairs.} Table~\ref{tab:ext-contradoc} gives the counts. Two of its findings
need comment:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{The committee confirms the verdict reading.} It judged \extCdSpotContradicted{} of
  the \extCdPairs{} inserted sentences contradicted by the rest of their document.
\item \textbf{DiscoScore RC and LC rise when a contradiction is inserted.} This is presumably
  because the inserted sentence repeats the nouns of the sentence it contradicts, which these
  scores reward as cohesion.
\end{itemize}
\emph{Exploratory.} With the inserted sentence false, the \extCdAssertEdgeNGpt{} graphs with a
found contradiction draw \extCdEvSupGpt{} relations to it, all of them supports and
\extCdEvConGpt{} conflicts. Every one of the \extCdVerdFallsGpt{} pairs whose score nevertheless
fell has no conflict on the inserted sentence: the two versions were mined independently, and
the rest of their graphs came out differently. The sign of each relation follows the truth
values, and the comparison across documents carries the miner's sampling noise. A paired design,
mining the clean document once and adding only the inserted claim's relations, would remove that
noise. With gpt-oss-120b, \extCdEvSupOss{} of the relations drawn to the false sentence
are supports and \extCdEvConOss{} a conflict (an inference drawn from a true claim to the false
one, which the sign rule correctly charges); \extCdVerdFallsNoConOss{} of its \extCdVerdFallsOss{}
verdict-world falls have no conflict on the inserted sentence.

\paragraph{Recall.} The GPT-6 arm finds the contradiction in \extCdRecallGpt\% of the 449
contradicted documents (gpt-oss-120b: \extCdRecallOss\%):
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
\item by edit: \extCdRecallInsertGpt\% of insertions and \extCdRecallReplaceGpt\% of
  replacements;
\item by scope: \extCdRecallGlobalGpt\% global
  {\scriptsize[\extCdRecallGlobalGptLo,\,\extCdRecallGlobalGptHi]}, \extCdRecallLocalGpt\% local
  {\scriptsize[\extCdRecallLocalGptLo,\,\extCdRecallLocalGptHi]} and \extCdRecallIntraGpt\%
  within a sentence {\scriptsize[\extCdRecallIntraGptLo,\,\extCdRecallIntraGptHi]};
\item by document type: \extCdRecallNewsGpt\% news, \extCdRecallStoryGpt\% stories and
  \extCdRecallWikiGpt\% Wikipedia.
\end{itemize}
A contradiction within one sentence is internal to a single claim at sentence granularity, so no
relation between two claims can find it; finer claims, as from an atomizer, could. A replacement
removes the sentence the new one would contradict, which leaves the miner less to link to. On the
442 clean documents, \extCdFpGpt\% of graphs contain at least one contradiction, the false-alarm
rate against which recall should be read; per claim pair, the rate is $\extCdFpPairGpt$
(gpt-oss-120b: \extCdFpOss\% of graphs, $\extCdFpPairOss$ per pair).

\subsection{Check 3: The Sign Follows the Truth}

ChatProtect's \extCpPairs{} pairs give \extCpSweepNGpt{} (relation, world) cases between the two
sentences. In every case the leave-one-out contribution of the relation is negative exactly when
the world falls in the relation's violated cells. This checks the implementation on natural
graphs and confirms Lemma~\ref{lem:edit} at work.

The GPT-6 arm finds the contradiction in \extCpConfStrongGpt\% of the pairs annotated as
contradictory and in \extCpConfNoneGpt\% of the others: low recall, no false alarms. With every
claim true, adding the second sentence lowers $\LCS$ on \extCpAssertDecGpt{} and raises it on
\extCpAssertIncGpt{} of \extCpAssertNGpt{} contradictory pairs ($p<\extCpAssertPGpt$). The split
by whether the contradiction was found is exploratory:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
\item where it was found, $\LCS$ falls on \extCpFoundFallGpt{} pairs and rises on
  \extCpFoundRiseGpt;
\item where it was not, it falls on \extCpNotFoundFallGpt{} and rises on \extCpNotFoundRiseGpt.
\end{itemize}
Every rise is a pair whose contradiction the miner missed; the score itself never moves the wrong
way.

gpt-oss-120b finds the contradiction in \extCpConfStrongOss\% and \extCpConfNoneOss\% of the two
groups, and its sweep covers \extCpSweepNOss{} cases, every one with the predicted sign. Where it
finds the contradiction, $\LCS$ falls on all \extCpFoundFallOss{} pairs, but it finds so few that
over all pairs falls and rises are even (\extCpAssertDecOss{} and \extCpAssertIncOss), so H3c
fails for it.

On the subset whose false side the annotators recorded, the contradictions found are
\extCpRealSupportGpt{} supports and \extCpRealConflictGpt{} conflicts at the real verdicts. A
self-contradiction between two false sentences, or between a true and a false one, is a support
at the verdict world. Counting contradictions would treat all of them as defects.

\paragraph{GDTB.} Each item gives the miner one relation's two arguments in their sentences. The
upper block of Table~\ref{tab:ext-gdtb} measures recall of the coupling; the lower block measures
the compile map's central claim, that contrast, concession and temporal sequence constrain
nothing. The difference between the two rates, \extGdCorrectGpt\% against \extGdNoneGpt\%, has a
95\% interval well above zero. The weakest categories are those the miner most often maps to a
neighbouring coupling: disjunction and restatement. The pre-registered \sense{Evidence} category
(PDTB \sense{Cause+Belief}) has no instance in the GDTB documents we use, so it is
absent from the table.

\paragraph{QT30.} Over \extQtWindows{} windows of the BBC debates, the GPT-6 arm reaches
pair-level precision, recall and F1 of \extQtPairPGpt, \extQtPairRGpt{} and \extQtPairFGpt, and a
coupling-level precision, recall and F1 of \extQtCoupPGpt, \extQtCoupRGpt{} and \extQtCoupFGpt.
gpt-oss-120b reaches \extQtPairFOss{} and \extQtCoupFOss, with
inference recall \extQtRecEntailmentOss\%. GPT-6's recall by coupling:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
\item inference: \extQtRecEntailmentGpt\% of \extQtNEntailment, with the direction right in
  \extQtDirGpt\% of those found;
\item rephrase: \extQtRecEquivalenceGpt\% of \extQtNEquivalence; another \extQtEqvAsEnt{} are
  mined as inferences;
\item conflict: \extQtRecContradictionGpt\% of \extQtNContradiction.
\end{itemize}
Inference Anchoring Theory annotates the inferences and conflicts that speakers' moves imply,
including across speakers' turns. Our miners are instructed to emit only relations
the text draws, which caps recall on this corpus by design.
