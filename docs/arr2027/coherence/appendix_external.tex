\paragraph{Protocol.} Every prediction, test and threshold was fixed in a pre-registration
committed before any graph was mined (\texttt{results/ext/PREREG.md}). The settings are:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
\item both graph miners, $K{=}5$, taxonomy v2, with the gateway cache bypassed;
\item $\LCS$ and $\Lambda$ with flat priors, $\Lambda$ exact by variable elimination on each
  connected component;
\item invariance within $\tau=0.01$;
\item two-sided exact sign tests and 95\% group-bootstrap intervals.
\end{itemize}
Two deviations were logged after the first results were read; both add analyses and remove none.
\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt,font=\normalfont\itshape]
\item[D1.] DiscoScore RC and LC count noun repetitions regardless of sentence order, so, like the
  length controls, they cannot change under a permutation. H1a and H1c are reported as
  registered and also without them (Table~\ref{tab:ext-order}).
\item[D2.] ContraDoc's clean documents were re-mined with fresh samples, to measure the miner's
  noise floor, as the LoCoBench test-split records already allow.
\end{description}

\paragraph{Check 1.} Gold-graph $\LCS$ is identical across every permutation, as it must be. With
GPT-6, the mined $\LCS$ falls on \extLocoPermDecGpt\% of the LoCoBench permutations, against
\extLocoPermDecJudge\% for the judge. Mined $\LCS$ moves less on LoCoBench than on ContraDoc,
whose documents are longer (median 39 claims against 14): with more claims, five sampled graphs
agree less. On SummEval, $\LCS$ correlates with consistency, fluency and relevance at
$\extSeRhoConsistencyGpt$, $\extSeRhoFluencyGpt$ and $\extSeRhoRelevanceGpt$, and with coherence
at $\extSeRhoCohGpt$ {\scriptsize[\extSeRhoCohGptLo,\,\extSeRhoCohGptHi]}. \extSeEmptyGpt\% of the
summaries, of about three sentences, have no factor relation at all.

\paragraph{Check 2.} Recall of the contradiction, the share of contradicted documents whose mined
graph has a contradiction or exclusion on the inserted or replaced claim, is
\extCdRecallGpt\% with GPT-6 and \extCdRecallOss\% with gpt-oss:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
\item by edit: \extCdRecallInsertGpt\% for insertions and \extCdRecallReplaceGpt\% for
  replacements;
\item by scope: \extCdRecallGlobalGpt\% global, \extCdRecallLocalGpt\% local and
  \extCdRecallIntraGpt\% within one sentence;
\item by document type: \extCdRecallNewsGpt\% news, \extCdRecallStoryGpt\% stories and
  \extCdRecallWikiGpt\% Wikipedia.
\end{itemize}
The pre-registered ordering by scope failed. A contradiction within one sentence is internal to a
single claim at sentence granularity, so no relation between two claims can find it. Replacements
remove the original sentence along with the text it contradicted, which leaves the miner less to
link to. On the clean documents, \extCdFpGpt\% of the mined graphs contain a contradiction (GPT-6).

\paragraph{Check 3.} ChatProtect's \extCpPairs{} pairs give \extCpSweepNGpt{} (relation, world)
cases with GPT-6 and \extCpSweepNOss{} with gpt-oss. Every one has the predicted sign, a check of
the implementation on natural graphs. The miner finds the contradiction in
\extCpConfStrongGpt\%/\extCpConfStrongOss\% (GPT-6/gpt-oss) of the contradictory pairs and in
\extCpConfNoneGpt\%/\extCpConfNoneOss\% of the others. With every claim true, adding the second
sentence lowers $\LCS$ on \extCpAssertDecGpt{} and raises it on \extCpAssertIncGpt{} of
\extCpAssertNGpt{} contradictory pairs (GPT-6; $p<\extCpAssertPGpt$); with gpt-oss, on
\extCpAssertDecOss{} and \extCpAssertIncOss.

\paragraph{Check 4.} On QT30, the GPT-6 miner's pair-level precision, recall and F1 are
\extQtPairPGpt, \extQtPairRGpt{} and \extQtPairFGpt, and its coupling-level F1 is
\extQtCoupFGpt{} (gpt-oss: \extQtPairFOss{} and \extQtCoupFOss). Recall by coupling:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
\item inference: \extQtRecEntailmentGpt\% of \extQtNEntailment;
\item rephrase: \extQtRecEquivalenceGpt\% of \extQtNEquivalence, of which \extQtEqvAsEnt{} are
  mined as inferences;
\item conflict: \extQtRecContradictionGpt\% of \extQtNContradiction.
\end{itemize}
The direction of a recovered inference is right in \extQtDirGpt\% of cases. QT30's annotators
reconstruct implicit inferences between speakers; our miners emit only the relations the text
draws, which caps recall here. Table~\ref{tab:ext-gdtb} breaks GDTB down by category.
