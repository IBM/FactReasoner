# Coherence paper: appendix section "LCS as a Function of Λ"

Status: PLAN, for review. Written 2026-11-24. Target: `docs/arr2027/coherence/main.tex`, which
gets a new appendix section, two one-clause pointers in the main text, and a new figure from a
new script.

## Why

The main text says LCS is "a strictly increasing function of Λ for a fixed claim set and prior"
(§2.6, "Analysing the score") and uses both scales ("One score, two scales", §4.1), but never
writes the function down. The appendix will give the map in closed form, its domain and range,
its inverse, its derivative and its limits, and show both scales side by side on the paper's own
data.

## Notation

- u₀ = π(x\*): the prior probability of the committed world. With flat priors, u₀ = 2⁻ⁿ.
- u = P_R(x\*): its probability after the relations.
- By Eq. `eq:lambda`, u = u₀ e^Λ.

## Derivation (what the section states and proves)

1. **Domain of Λ.** Since 0 < u ≤ 1, Λ ∈ (−∞, −log u₀]; with flat priors the top is n log 2. The
   lower end is finite for every graph: by Prop. `prop:sat`(i), Λ ≥ Σ_{conflicts} log(1 − p_r).
   It reaches −∞ only in the limit of a conflict of weight 1. So Λ is unbounded below, while its
   upper bound depends on the claim set.
2. **The map, closed form.** Substituting u = u₀e^Λ in Eq. `eq:lcs`:

   LCS = σ( Λ − log[(1 − u)/(1 − u₀)] ) = e^Λ(1 − u₀) / ( 1 + e^Λ(1 − 2u₀) ).

   - The first form is "the logistic of Λ, corrected by how far the relations have moved the
     probability of not-x\*".
   - The second form is explicit.
   - Checked numerically: it equals the implementation (`odds_lcs`) to within 10⁻¹⁵ over 20,000
     random cases.
3. **Range and anchors.**
   - Λ = 0 ⇒ LCS = ½.
   - Λ → −∞ ⇒ LCS → 0.
   - Λ → −log u₀ (x\* forced, u → 1) ⇒ LCS → 1.

   So LCS maps the whole domain of Λ onto (0, 1).
4. **Monotonicity, with the derivative.** dLCS/dΛ = LCS(1 − LCS)/(1 − u) > 0. So for a fixed u₀
   the map is a strictly increasing bijection, which proves the main-text claim in one line, and
   every ordering statement about Λ transfers.
5. **Inverse.** Λ = log LCS − log[(1 − LCS) − u₀(1 − 2·LCS)]. As u₀ → 0 this tends to logit(LCS).
   The implementation will use the numerically stable log1p form.
6. **Relation to σ(Λ).** The correction term log[(1 − u₀)/(1 − u)] is ≥ 0 exactly when Λ ≥ 0. So:
   - LCS ≥ σ(Λ) for Λ ≥ 0, and LCS ≤ σ(Λ) for Λ ≤ 0: **LCS is always at least as far from ½ as
     σ(Λ)**.
   - LCS → σ(Λ) as u₀ → 0 with u bounded away from 1, which is the regime of the ladders (n = 12–14,
     u₀ ≈ 10⁻⁴).
   - This explains the main text's remark that an untouched claim "moves LCS slightly towards
     σ(Λ)": it halves u₀ and leaves Λ unchanged.
7. **Compression: why two scales.** Near either end the slope is ≈ LCS(1 − LCS)/(1 − u). A step
   ΔΛ therefore moves LCS by at most about LCS(1 − LCS)·ΔΛ/(1 − u), so resolving an increase on the
   [0, 1] scale needs ΔΛ ≳ τ(1 − u)/[LCS(1 − LCS)]. This gives the reason behind two facts the main
   text already reports, with no new numbers:
   - the 17 unresolved gold increases, all at LCS < 0.06 or > 0.9;
   - the 13-nat step at LCS ≈ 10⁻¹⁷ that moves LCS by 5 × 10⁻¹².
8. **Additivity is a property of Λ only.** For relations on disjoint claim sets, Λ adds
   (Prop. `prop:basic`(iii)). LCS of the union is the map applied to Λ₁ + Λ₂ with u₀ = u₀,₁·u₀,₂,
   which is not a function of LCS₁ and LCS₂ alone. A two-line example shows this.

## Figure: `figures/fig_lcs_lambda.pdf` (full width, 2 panels)

- **(a) The map.** LCS against Λ for flat priors with n = 2, 5 and 14 claims (a 3-step ordinal
  ramp of one hue, validated), plus the u₀ → 0 limit σ(Λ) as a dashed grey reference.
  - Each curve ends at its domain bound Λ = n log 2, where LCS = 1; a dot marks the bound.
  - The x-axis runs over about [−8, 10], with reference lines at Λ = 0 and LCS = ½.
- **(b) Both scales on the ladders.** Every rung of the LoCoBench test split, gold graphs and the
  GPT-6 miner's graphs (two categorical colours, labelled), plotted as Λ (x) against LCS (y).
  - The points lie on the n = 12–14 curves, showing Λ spread over tens of nats while LCS is squeezed
    against 0 and 1.
  - Marginal rugs on both axes show the two distributions side by side.
  - Large negative Λ values (pairwise miners reach far lower) are clipped at the axis edge with a
    note, or the axis is symlog; choose whichever reads better when rendered.
