# ConflictBank incoherent corpus, v2 — iteration log and findings

Generated by `scripts/gen_incoherent_responses.py --pattern invented-subtle`, evaluated by
`scripts/eval_incoherent_judges.py`. This directory is an **iteration record**, not a
finished corpus: the acceptance gate is not yet met, and the reasons are the interesting
part.

## The goal and why it is hard

The v1 corpus (`../incoherent/`) scores low on LCS but an LLM judge catches it just as
easily (`judge_direct` 0.25), because every passage announces its own contradiction. v2
aims for realistic prose that LCS still scores low while a judge does not.

Those two requirements pull against each other, and the pull is structural:

> What makes a conflict visible to a **pairwise** relation miner is an explicit textual
> contradiction between two claims — which is exactly what makes it visible to a judge
> reading the same prose.

## → This became a paper contribution

Finding 1 is now **Proposition 4 ("Jointly inconsistent, pairwise consistent")** in
`docs/iclr2027/coherence` §10 (Limitations), replacing what had been an unsupported
one-sentence assertion. The paper previously claimed *"three claims can be jointly
inconsistent with no two of them pairwise inconsistent, and our model cannot represent
that"* with no proof and no example; it now carries a proof, an exact quantification, and
this measured instance.

Reproduce with `python scripts/kary_representability.py`; pinned by
`tests/test_kary_representability.py` (11 tests).

    model                       P(1,1,1)  P(a1&a2)  P(a1&a3)  P(a2&a3)
    no factors                    0.1250    0.2500    0.2500    0.2500
    pairwise (1,1) -> 0.05        0.0000    0.0121    0.0121    0.0121
    pairwise (1,1) -> 0.0         0.0000    0.0000    0.0000    0.0000
    TERNARY off (1,1,1)           0.0000    0.1429    0.1429    0.1429

Only the ternary row achieves the intended semantics. A seeded search over 2x10^5 free
pairwise parameterizations gets `P(1,1,1)` below 1e-4 only by dragging pair-true mass to
0.028, against the ternary's 0.143.

## Finding 1: a planted conflict must be exactly TWO contradictory atoms

LCS's model (Def. 1) admits only pairwise factors ψ(a_s, a_t). A conflict that needs three
claims at once is **unrepresentable**, not merely missed. Measured on iteration 1, one
instance per conflict type:

| instance | type | operands after atomization | conflict edges mined |
|---|---|---|---|
| cb-000 | numeric | **3 atoms**: "356 teams" / "32 conferences" / "10 per conference" | **0** |
| cb-001 | chronology | 2 atoms, but needs date algebra | **0** |
| cb-002 | quantifier | **2 atoms**: general rule vs violating instance | **3** (p=0.94) |

I reached this only after refuting two wrong diagnoses, both worth recording:

1. *"The coupling estimator cannot do arithmetic."* **Refuted** — fed the pair directly it
   returned `contradiction p=0.95` for numeric and `p=0.96` for quantifier.
2. *"`window=4` is too narrow, the atoms are too far apart."* **Refuted** — re-mining
   cb-000 with `window=8` gave 0 conflicts, and with `all_pairs` (every pair compared, 28
   relations, 240 s) **still 0**.

The actual cause, confirmed by testing all three pairs among the operand atoms: every pair
returns `NO RELATION` or `entailment`, never `contradiction`. 356 ≠ 32×10 is a three-way
relation.

**Applied:** `--conflict-type auto` now emits `quantifier` only. `numeric` and `chronology`
remain selectable for ablation and are documented as non-representable. Chronology is a
separate, genuine estimator blind spot: it returns NO RELATION even in isolation.

## Finding 2: the llama judge is saturated, not fooled

The obvious headline — "both llama judges score 1.000, completely fooled" — does not
survive the control. Scoring the *matched coherent* passages on the same judges:

| judge | v2 iter1 | v2 iter2 | coherent control | gap (control − incoherent) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `llama.judge_direct` | 1.000 | 1.000 | **1.000** | **+0.000** |
| `llama.judge_geval` | 1.000 | 1.000 | **1.000** | **+0.000** |
| `claude.judge_direct` | 0.417 | 0.250 | 0.667 | +0.417 |
| `claude.judge_geval` | 0.417 | 0.417 | 0.667 | +0.250 |

`llama-3-3-70b` rates *any* fluent encyclopedic passage 5/5, coherent or not. It is not
being deceived; it carries **no signal** on this material, so a "we beat the judge
baseline" claim resting on it would be an artifact of an uninformative judge. Claude
discriminates and is **not** fooled — and iteration 2's more-visible quantifier conflict
pushed `claude.judge_direct` *down* to 0.250.

Any judge claim must therefore be reported against the control, per judge model. A judge
that cannot separate the classes is not evidence in either direction.

## Finding 3: llama misses even a contradiction the passage spells out

The saturation is worse than "insensitive to subtle conflicts". Scoring four texts on
`llama-3-3-70b`, `judge_direct`:

| text | llama | Claude |
|---|---|---|
| coherent control | 1.000 | 0.667 |
| v2 iter2 (subtle quantifier) | 1.000 | 0.250 |
| **v1, explicit bridge** ("the two statements are mutually exclusive… Therefore…") | **1.000** | **0.250** |
| shipped fixture `biography-contradicted` (five planted contradictions) | 0.250 | 0.000 |

llama rates the v1 corpus a perfect 5/5 — the same text Claude rates 0.25 — even though
that passage *states its own contradiction in so many words*. It only drops when a fixture
carries five separate planted contradictions.

So `llama.judge_geval = 1.000` was never evidence about the corpus. On single-conflict
passages llama is not a usable judge baseline in either direction, and the "easily fooled
judge" result cannot be built on it.

## Finding 4: iteration 2 fails the LCS side

Quantifier-only generation fixed the representability problem but lost the score separation:

| instance | conflict edges | `mean_marginal` | `consistency` | `log_partition` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| cb-000 | **0** | 0.682 | 0.906 | 1.000 |
| cb-001 | 1 | 0.741 | 0.835 | 0.846 |
| cb-002 | 3 | 0.749 | 0.605 | 0.607 |
| *coherent control (n=11, v1 run)* | *~0* | *0.737* | *0.845* | *0.970* |

`mean_marginal` 0.682–0.749 straddles the control mean of 0.737 — i.e. **LCS does not
separate these from coherent text.** One planted conflict inside a 15–20-atom passage moves
the mean of 15–20 marginals too little to register. The v1 corpus separated (0.539 vs
0.737) because its passages were half the length *and* saturated with conflict edges
(13/13 instances).

That is the quantitative form of the tension: dilution. Realistic prose needs length and
supporting detail; `mean_marginal` averages over every atom, so one defect among twenty
atoms is washed out regardless of how well the miner sees it. `consistency` (0.605 on the
3-conflict instance) and the conflict-edge count degrade more gracefully and are the
readouts to use for this material.

## Cost, measured

| stage | cost |
|---|---|
| generation (Claude) | ~5 s/instance |
| atomization + windowed mining | **~145 s/instance** (v2 passages are ~1300 chars → 15–19 atoms) |
| Merlin inference | 0.2 s |
| judges (2 judges × 5 seeds × 2 models) | ~20 calls/instance |

Mining dominates and grows with passage length. Use `--no-lcs` while iterating on the
prompt; score a sample separately. A 3-instance LCS run once sat blocked on network I/O
with ~0 CPU for 40 minutes — that is cost, not a hang.

## Status: the stated target is not reachable as specified

Two independent measurements say so, and both are about the instruments rather than the
prompt:

1. **No judge is simultaneously discriminating and fooled.** llama is saturated (1.000 on
   coherent control, on subtle conflicts, and on text that spells out its own
   contradiction). Claude discriminates and was not fooled by any of **twelve** constructions
   — nine hand-written probes plus three generated iterations.
2. **`mean_marginal` dilutes.** Realistic prose needs 15–20 atoms; one planted conflict
   moves a 15–20-term average too little to separate from the control (0.68–0.75 vs 0.737).
   The v1 corpus separated only because it was short *and* conflict-saturated.

What is achievable, and what I would propose instead:

* **Report `consistency` and the conflict-edge count, not `mean_marginal`,** on realistic
  long-form text. On the 3-conflict instance `consistency` reached 0.605 against a 0.845
  control — that readout does separate.
* **Use Claude as the judge baseline** and accept that it is not fooled. "LCS localizes the
  defect per-claim while a judge returns one uninformative scalar" is a defensible claim;
  "the judge is fooled" is not.
* **The pairwise-representability limit is the most publishable finding here** — a conflict
  needing three claims (356 ≠ 32×10) is invisible to LCS by construction, verified with
  `all_pairs`. That is a real statement about the model in the paper.

Deliverables that stand regardless: the three-pattern generator, the judge/LCS evaluation
harness, 42 tests, and the four findings above.

- v1 pattern reproducible via `--pattern assert-both-then-negate`.
- `control` pattern added for the matched coherent baseline (required: LCS has no absolute
  zero). Note controls mine 0 conflict edges, which pins `consistency_conflict` and
  `log_partition` at exactly 1.000 **by construction**, not by evidence.
