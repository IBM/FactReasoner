# LoCoBench validation study: annotator brief

Please read this page in full before the practice projects. The whole study takes about
**3.5-4 hours**, in sessions of at most 90 minutes with a break between them. Never work longer than
five hours in total; if you are running long, stop and tell the coordinator.

You will do two tasks, always in this order: **Task 1** (filler reading, about 75 minutes) and
then **Task 2** (response comparison, about 2 hours). Before either, do the two **practice**
projects (about 20 minutes) and join the 15-minute calibration discussion.

## Ground rules (these matter for the paper)

- Work **alone**. Do not discuss study items with anyone until everyone has finished, except in
  the calibration discussion of the practice items.
- **Do not open the data files** (`data/locobench-*`, the key, the generation logs) or the
  paper's examples while the study is running, and do not look items up. Everything you need is
  on the screen.
- Judge what the **text says**, not what you know about the topic. Several claims in these
  answers are deliberately false; that is part of the design.
- Answer every screen in order. Do not skip, and do not go back to change earlier answers.
- If a screen is genuinely unclear, give your best answer, set confidence to 1, and write a
  note in the reason box (Task 2) or the quote box (Task 1).

## Task 1: filler reading

Each screen shows one paragraph of an expert-style answer with **one or two sentences
highlighted**, and two claims, **X** and **Y**, which the highlighted text is about.

1. **Connection.** What connection does the *highlighted* text itself draw between X and Y?
   Pick the one that fits best:
   - *X is given as a reason, cause or evidence for Y* ("X, which shows that Y"; "because X,
     Y"; "X led to Y"). The reverse direction is the next option.
   - *X and Y cannot both be true* ("X cannot be squared with Y"; "X rules out Y").
   - *X and Y say the same thing*; *exactly one holds*; *at least one holds*.
   - *Only contrasted, conceded, or ordered in time*: the text sets them side by side ("whereas",
     "although", "before") without making one depend on the other.
   - *No connection*: both are stated, but nothing links them.
   A connection that is merely plausible from your own knowledge does not count. Only what the
   text says counts.
2. **Stance on X and on Y.** Does the highlighted text *assert* the claim (state it as true),
   *deny* it (say or imply it is false, for example "rules out the claim that ..."), or *not
   state* it at all?
3. **Other statements.** Does the highlighted text *also* connect X or Y to some *other*
   statement, one that is neither X nor Y? If yes, quote the connecting words.
4. **Confidence.**

## Task 2: response comparison

Each screen shows a question, a box listing which claims in the answers are **false** (take this
as given, even if you believe otherwise), and two complete answers, **A** and **B**. The two
answers are identical except for the **highlighted** sentences.

Decide which answer is **more logically coherent given those facts**: which one's claims fit
together better, and whose stated connections hold up. Typical differences:

- one answer sets two **true** claims against each other ("X cannot be squared with Y") where
  the other just states them;
- one answer draws a sound inference between true claims that the other leaves out;
- one answer **derives** a false claim from a true one, the other leaves them unconnected or uses
  the true claim to **refute** the false one;
- the two answers say the same thing in different words.

Choose **About equal** when the highlighted difference does not make either answer more or less
coherent. That is a real answer, not a fallback.

The same answer appears in several consecutive screens with different highlighted parts. Read it
carefully the first time; on later screens you only need to read the highlighted sentences and
whatever they refer to.

**Not the target:** length, fluency, style, vocabulary, how interesting the answer is, or whether
you agree with its conclusion. Do not reward an answer for being longer or for adding a
connective.

## Timing

Label Studio records the time per screen. As a guide: about 1 minute per Task 1 screen; about
3 minutes for the first screen of a new question in Task 2 and 30-45 seconds for each of its
other screens.
